# Guide: setting up the Film Lab on your phone

Three parts: switch on GitHub Pages (once, on any computer or phone), install the Lab on your
phone (once), then develop a test roll.

## Part 1 — Switch on GitHub Pages (once)

GitHub Pages turns files in this repository into a web page. That's how your phone will open the
Lab.

**What this means for privacy:** the Lab's page becomes reachable by anyone who has the link.
It contains only the app itself — your photos are never uploaded and never pass through it. The
repository is already public, so no code becomes visible that isn't already.

1. Open the repository on GitHub: **https://github.com/badgermode/Canon.jpeg**
2. Click the **Settings** tab (along the top, on the right; on a phone you may need to scroll the
   tab bar sideways). You need to be signed in as the repository's owner.
3. In the left-hand menu, under **Code and automation**, click **Pages**.
4. Under **Build and deployment**:
   - **Source:** choose **Deploy from a branch**.
   - **Branch:** open the first drop-down and choose
     **`claude/digital-film-camera-planning-ivrhh0`** (the branch the Lab lives on for now).
     Leave the second drop-down on **`/ (root)`**.
   - Click **Save**.
5. Wait one to two minutes, then reload the page. A box at the top should say
   **"Your site is live at https://badgermode.github.io/Canon.jpeg/"**.

If the menus look different from this description, GitHub has rearranged them — tell me what
you see and I'll adjust the steps.

**Later:** when this work is merged into the `main` branch, come back here and switch the branch
to `main`. Until then, every change I push to the branch above appears on the site within a
couple of minutes.

## Part 2 — Install the Lab on your phone (once)

1. On your Android phone, open **Chrome** and go to
   **https://badgermode.github.io/Canon.jpeg/** — it takes you straight to the Film Lab.
2. Tap Chrome's **⋮** menu (top right), then **Add to Home screen** (on some phones it says
   **Install app**). Confirm.
3. Open **Film Lab** from your home screen. It opens full-screen, like an app.
4. At the bottom of the Lab it should say **works offline**. To check: switch on aeroplane mode,
   close the Lab, open it again. It should still open.

**Updates:** whenever you open the Lab with an internet connection, it picks up my latest
changes automatically.

## Part 3 — Develop a test roll

The camera hasn't arrived, so use photos from your phone's camera.

1. In the Lab, **Kodak Portra 400** is selected (it's the only film so far).
2. Tap **Choose photos**. Your phone's picker opens. Press and hold one photo to start selecting,
   then tap others to add them. 10 to 36 photos is a good test — try people, daylight outdoors,
   and an indoor scene.
3. Tap **Develop roll** and watch it develop. Keep the Lab open; the screen stays on by itself.
4. You'll see the **contact sheet** first, then all the frames below it.
   - Tap a frame to view it full-screen. Swap between frames with **‹ ›**.
   - **Hold** the **Hold to see original** button to compare with the photo before developing.
   - **Save** saves that one photo.
5. **Save roll (ZIP)** saves everything — numbered photos, the contact sheet, and a small text
   file describing the roll — as one ZIP file in your **Downloads** folder.
   **Save contact sheet** saves just the contact sheet as a picture.

Good to know:

- Photos are developed in **name order**. Phone photos are usually named by date and time, so
  that's the order they were taken.
- Phone photos are scaled down to the A590's size (3264 pixels across) and cropped to the 35mm
  frame shape (3:2), so what you see is close to what the real camera will produce.
- Upright photos lie sideways on the contact sheet, exactly as they would on a strip of negatives.
- The developed photos don't keep the original's hidden details (date taken, camera settings).
  That's on the list for later.
